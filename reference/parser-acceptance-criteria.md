# Parser Acceptance Criteria: an industry-derived checklist

**Purpose**: This is the vendor-evaluation checklist called for in `MISSION.md`'s success criteria ("I hold a vendor-evaluation checklist I can apply to a new OCR product within an hour"). It is synthesized entirely from the six research files in `reference/parsing-landscape/`, which cover resume-parsing engines, ATS platforms, job boards, and big tech. Every claim below is traced to the file it came from and, where that file itself had a primary external source, to that original source.

**How to read the evidence tiers**: three tiers are used throughout —
- **Vendor-claimed**: stated by the company selling the product, no independent replication found by the source research.
- **Independently measured**: a third party (researcher, platform not selling the tool, published benchmark) measured it.
- **Unverified / not disclosed**: neither party has published a number; the research files explicitly flagged this.

Do not treat "vendor-claimed" numbers as ground truth when scoring your own parser — see the Gaps section at the end.

---

## 1. Input format handling

A parser that wants to be industry-standard must handle the input diversity that real platforms report, and must be honest about what it will silently mangle.

- **Minimum format set observed across the industry**: DOC, DOCX, PDF, RTF, TXT, HTML, ODT at minimum; several vendors go further (Textkernel claims 70+ formats; RChilli and HireAbility add DOTX/DOTM/PNG/JPEG). Source: `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md` (citing [Textkernel product page](https://www.textkernel.com/products-solutions/parser/) and [Textkernel API docs](https://developer.textkernel.com/Parser/master/)); `engines-hireability-rchilli.md` (citing [RChilli product docs](https://www.rchilli.com/documentation/product-docs/resume-parser)).
- **DOCX is the most reliable format industry-wide, PDF is second-tier**. iCIMS historically preferred DOCX for this reason; Taleo's own guidance says PDFs built in design tools (Canva/InDesign) frequently produce "jumbled or incomplete extraction" because text is embedded as images; BambooHR's third-party integration partners recommend DOCX as "the safest format." Source: `ats-icims-smartrecruiters-ashby-bamboohr-taleo.md` (citing [Taleo Resume Format Guide](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/taleo-resume-format-guide), a secondary source, and [Oracle Taleo Candidate File Import docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/21b/otcug/candidate-file-import.html) for the "third-party partner service" framing).
- **Text-layer vs. image-layer PDF is the fault line, not "PDF" as a format**. Greenhouse only supports PDFs with a selectable text layer; a scanned or flattened PDF (or one exported from Canva/Figma with a scrambled text layer) is treated as unparseable. Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md` (citing [Resume Optimizer Pro: Greenhouse ATS Resume Format](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/greenhouse-ats-resume-format), a secondary source; the parsing-approach claim itself cites [Jobloo](https://jobloo.co/blog/how-ats-systems-read-your-resume/)).
- **Multi-column layouts are the single most commonly cited failure mode** across the entire landscape:
  - Workday: two-column PDFs cause "data interleaving" where adjacent-column text merges into a scrambled string; Workday's own admin guide recommends resumes "that don't have images or image-based styles." Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md` (citing [Resume Optimizer Pro: Workday Resume Format](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/workday-resume-format) and [Workday Documentation](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/set-up-prospects-and-candidates/hdc1552497830785.html)).
  - Ashby: explicitly documents that multi-column layouts cause "text interleaving," potentially dropping structured data like skills tags — despite Ashby's LLM-based approach being marketed as more layout-tolerant. Source: `ats-icims-smartrecruiters-ashby-bamboohr-taleo.md` (citing [How Does Ashby Work](https://enhancv.com/blog/how-does-ashby-work/), a secondary source).
  - Textkernel measured this at scale: ~40% of resumes have multi-column or complex layouts, and its shift from rule-based to a gradient-boosting classifier for column detection moved visual-gap classification from 82%→91% and column-separator detection from 60%→82% on well-rendered documents, tested across 12,000+ CVs. This is the one **independently-measured, methodology-disclosed** multi-column number in the whole corpus (it's the vendor's own internal benchmark, but it discloses sample size and before/after methodology, which most vendor claims below do not). Source: `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md` (citing [Textkernel blog: Improving Extraction from Column Resumes](https://www.textkernel.com/learn-support/blog/improving-extraction-from-column-resumes/)).
  - Indeed cites a general industry claim that modern parsers solve multi-column reading via the XY-Cut algorithm, but that "older government and legacy enterprise systems (Taleo, older SuccessFactors) still struggle." Source: `job-boards-linkedin-indeed-zip-glassdoor.md` (citing [Enhancv: The State of Resume Parsing](https://enhancv.com/blog/ats-resume-parsing/), a secondary source).
- **Acceptance criterion**: a parser should be scored separately on (a) DOCX single-column, (b) text-layer PDF single-column, (c) text-layer PDF multi-column, (d) image/scanned PDF. Treating "PDF support" as one bucket hides where real systems actually fail.

## 2. Extraction approach: text-extraction-first, OCR as fallback

The most important structural finding across the six files is that **most high-volume, real-world platforms do NOT run OCR as their primary path** — they extract the existing text layer and reserve OCR for the minority of documents that need it.

- **Workday** uses Apache Tika (an open-source text-extraction library) for its core pipeline — converting the file to plain text with layout stripped, then running entity extraction on that text. OCR ("Document Intelligence") is a separate, optional add-on via Workday Extend/AI Gateway, not part of core parsing. Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md` (citing [Resume Optimizer Pro: Workday Resume Format](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/workday-resume-format) and [Workday Marketplace: Document Intelligence](https://marketplace-setup.workday.com/apps/425535/document-intelligence-with-the-ai-gateway/overview)).
- **Ashby** explicitly states it does **not** use OCR — it reads resumes with an LLM rather than "scanning images," a deliberate architectural choice framed as a differentiator. Source: `ats-icims-smartrecruiters-ashby-bamboohr-taleo.md` (citing [Ashby AI Features documentation](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/ai-features-in-ashby)).
- **Greenhouse** distinguishes itself as "API-based... NOT OCR-based" and explicitly requires a selectable text layer; when that's absent it does not fall back to OCR, it drops the candidate (see Section 6). Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md` (citing [Jobloo](https://jobloo.co/blog/how-ats-systems-read-your-resume/)).
- **Textkernel** runs a three-stage pipeline — (1) convert to plain text, (2) verify the text is usable, (3) parse — and only invokes OCR when it detects a scanned/photographed document, which it says is ~5% of documents. Textkernel's own docs state: "The vast majority of problems in parsing are not from processing the plain text, but from conversion to plain text" — i.e., the hard part is text extraction, not NLP on top of it. OCR is capped at 10 pages and a 120-second timeout, and explicitly does not affect response time for non-image documents. Source: `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md` (citing [Textkernel Developer Docs](https://developer.textkernel.com/tx-platform/v9/resume-parser/overview/getting-started/) and [OCR addon page](https://www.textkernel.com/resume-parsing-ocr-addon/)).
- **RChilli and Daxtra** are the counter-examples in this corpus: both market OCR as integral (RChilli: "hybrid OCR + text extraction... unified API" for both scanned and native PDFs; Daxtra: OCR for JPG/JPEG/PNG). Neither, however, discloses what fraction of their real traffic actually needs OCR, so it's unclear if they differ from Textkernel's "5% fallback" model in practice or just market it differently. Source: `engines-hireability-rchilli.md` (citing [RChilli blog: Parse Scanned Resumes with RChilli OCR Technology](https://www.rchilli.com/blog/parse-scanned-resumes-with-rchilli-ocr-technology)); `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md` (citing [Daxtra product page](https://www.daxtra.com/products/resume-parsing-software/)).
- **Indeed's own patent** (US10296872, "computer vision and OCR... in combination with a user feedback interface") frames OCR as needed specifically for scanned/image resumes, with a two-pass system where user feedback corrects bounding-box regrouping — i.e., even Indeed treats OCR output as needing a human-correction loop, not a one-shot pipeline. Source: `job-boards-linkedin-indeed-zip-glassdoor.md` (citing [US Patent 10296872](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10296872)).
- **Acceptance criterion**: architect the parser as text-extraction-first with an OCR fallback gated on a text-quality check (e.g., "does the extracted layer contain a plausible density of real words"), not as OCR-always. This matches the dominant real-world pattern (Workday/Tika, Textkernel's 3-stage pipeline, Ashby's explicit avoidance) rather than the minority OCR-first marketing pattern (RChilli/Daxtra).

## 4. Latency / throughput targets

- **RChilli**: ~300ms average parsing speed, framed alongside "1 resume/second throughput" and a claimed scale of 4.1 billion documents/year. **Vendor-claimed**, benchmark methodology not published. Source: `engines-hireability-rchilli.md`, citing [RChilli: Top 7 Benchmarks](https://www.rchilli.com/blog/top-7-benchmarks-for-comparing-resume-parsing-technology) and [RChilli markets page](https://www.rchilli.com/markets/ats-jobboards).
- **Textkernel**: "lightning-speed parsing in just 0.5 seconds," 2+ billion resumes/job postings processed annually. **Vendor-claimed**, no methodology. Source: `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md`, citing [Textkernel parser page](https://www.textkernel.com/products-solutions/parser/).
- **LinkedIn**: ~200 profile edits/second at <100ms latency, achieved via knowledge distillation shrinking a BERT model by 80% without accuracy loss — the one figure in this category that comes with disclosed engineering methodology (distillation technique named, tradeoff stated) even though it is still a vendor/platform self-report. Source: `job-boards-linkedin-indeed-zip-glassdoor.md`, citing [LinkedIn Engineering Blog: Extracting skills from content to fuel the LinkedIn Skills Graph](https://engineering.linkedin.com/blog/2023/extracting-skills-from-content-to-fuel-the-linkedin-skills-graph).
- **Textkernel OCR-specific limits**: capped at 10 pages, 120-second timeout per OCR request — useful as a concrete ceiling for what "acceptable OCR latency" looks like in a production system, even absent an accuracy number. Source: `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md`, citing [Textkernel Developer Docs](https://developer.textkernel.com/tx-platform/v9/resume-parser/overview/getting-started/).
- **Acceptance criterion**: target sub-second latency for the text-extraction path (LinkedIn and Textkernel both land near or under 500ms–1s), with an explicit, separately-budgeted ceiling (not the same SLA) for the OCR fallback path — Textkernel's 120s/10-page cap is a reasonable reference ceiling to adopt or beat.

## 6. Failure-mode handling

This is the category with the clearest anti-pattern in the whole corpus.

- **Greenhouse: silent drop on parse failure.** When parsing fails, "candidate is not added to pipeline at all" — no partial record, no flag to the recruiter, no manual-review queue mentioned. This is the anti-pattern to explicitly design against: a resume that fails to parse should never silently vanish from the pipeline. Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md`, citing [Jobloo: How ATS Systems Read Your Resume](https://jobloo.co/blog/how-ats-systems-read-your-resume/) (secondary source; no official Greenhouse documentation confirms this failure behavior in primary form, which is itself worth noting when evaluating the claim's strength).
- **Workday: degraded-but-visible failure.** Multi-column PDFs don't get dropped, but produce a scrambled, unreadable candidate profile in the recruiter dashboard — a "silent corruption" failure mode distinct from Greenhouse's "silent omission." Source: `ats-greenhouse-lever-workday.md`, citing [Resume Optimizer Pro: Workday Resume Format](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/workday-resume-format).
- **Indeed's patented approach is the explicit counter-example**: a two-pass system where the first pass extracts text blocks and a second pass uses **user feedback** to regroup bounding boxes for correction — i.e., a designed human-in-the-loop recovery path rather than silent failure. Source: `job-boards-linkedin-indeed-zip-glassdoor.md`, citing [US Patent 10296872](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10296872).
- **SmartRecruiters** returns explicit machine-readable error codes (`UNPARSABLE_RESUME` for image files, `INCOMPLETE_RESUME` when required fields are missing) rather than failing silently — a pattern worth adopting for API design. Source: `ats-icims-smartrecruiters-ashby-bamboohr-taleo.md`, citing [SmartRecruiters Parse Resume API reference](https://developers.smartrecruiters.com/reference/candidatesresumeparse).
- **Acceptance criterion**: define and test three distinct failure modes separately — (1) hard failure (nothing extracted: must never silently drop the candidate; should raise a typed error and/or route to manual review, as SmartRecruiters' error codes model); (2) soft failure (partial/garbled extraction, as in Workday's column interleaving: must be flagged, not presented as a complete record); (3) low-confidence extraction (values extracted but uncertain: should carry a confidence score, not be presented identically to high-confidence fields). None of the six platforms studied publish a confidence-score mechanism, so this is a place the checklist recommends going beyond observed industry practice, not just matching it.

## 7. Compliance / security

- **RChilli** is the only vendor in the corpus that discloses a compliance/certification stack: ISO 27001, SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, and FedRAMP are referenced in its markets/enterprise materials. Source: `engines-hireability-rchilli.md` — note: the specific certification list is drawn from RChilli's own markets pages ([https://www.rchilli.com/markets/ats-crm-erp](https://www.rchilli.com/markets/ats-crm-erp), [https://www.rchilli.com/markets/ats-jobboards](https://www.rchilli.com/markets/ats-jobboards)); the research file does not show a dedicated compliance/trust page being cited, so treat the specific certification list as vendor-claimed and verify directly against RChilli's current compliance documentation before relying on it.
- **No other vendor or platform in the six files discloses a compliance certification stack for resume parsing specifically.** Textkernel, Daxtra, HireAbility, and the ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters, Ashby, BambooHR, Taleo) each likely carry SOC 2 or equivalent as general SaaS vendors, but none of that is documented in the parsing-landscape research — it is out of scope of what was researched, not confirmed absent.
- **Ashby's PII-redaction-for-bias-reduction** (redacting name/address before AI evaluation) is the one compliance-adjacent design pattern found outside RChilli, framed as a bias-mitigation feature rather than a certification. Source: `ats-icims-smartrecruiters-ashby-bamboohr-taleo.md`, citing [Ashby AI Features documentation](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/ai-features-in-ashby).
- **Acceptance criterion**: treat RChilli's certification list as the ceiling reference for what an enterprise buyer will ask for (ISO 27001, SOC 2, HIPAA if touching health-adjacent data, GDPR if processing EU candidates, FedRAMP if selling to US government), but verify current status directly with RChilli or your own vendor rather than citing this checklist as proof. This is also directly relevant to the workspace's own standing rule: "Never commit real resumes (PII)... real samples live in `data/private/`" — the same PII-handling discipline that Ashby's redaction pattern and RChilli's certifications point at applies to the learner's own lab data.

For a learner or small team evaluating build-vs-buy, this is a signal to default toward "buy/license" unless you have LinkedIn-or-Workday-scale reasons and engineering capacity to build and maintain your own pipeline — and even then, expect to re-derive engineering effort like Textkernel's column-detection classifier (Section 1) from scratch.

## Gaps / what real-world validation would require

This checklist is **directional, not validating**. Applying it to a new OCR/parsing product will tell you what to *ask* and what categories of failure to *test for*, but it cannot yet tell you whether a given product's numbers are actually good, because:

1. **No shared, independently-verified benchmark exists anywhere in this research.** Every accuracy number in Section 3 is either vendor-self-reported with no disclosed methodology (RChilli 95–99%, Daxtra ~90%, SmartRecruiters +98%) or a secondary-source claim not attributed to any specific tested system (Enhancv's 92–97%/80–90%/40–60% tiers). The task brief that generated this checklist referenced a ZipRecruiter measured 73%/45% precision/recall figure — that figure does **not** appear in `job-boards-linkedin-indeed-zip-glassdoor.md`, whose ZipRecruiter section is marked "(unverified)" throughout. If that number exists, it needs to be found and cited from a primary source before this checklist can use it; right now it should be treated as absent from the evidence base, not as an established data point.
2. **No cross-vendor head-to-head exists.** `engines-textkernel-daxtra.md` says explicitly: "No primary-source head-to-head accuracy comparison between Textkernel and Daxtra"; the same gap holds across every other vendor pair in the corpus.
3. **No public resume test set exists.** Both major vendors "keep test suites proprietary; no public benchmark dataset for resume parsing exists as a primary reference" (`engines-textkernel-daxtra.md`). This directly motivates MISSION.md's second success criterion — "I have a resume test set with ground truth (PDF and DOCX) and can score any technique on it: CER, reading order, field-level F1" — because without your own ground-truth set, none of the vendor percentages above are comparable to each other or to your own parser.
4. **Failure-mode claims rest on secondary sources.** Greenhouse's silent-drop behavior (Section 6) — arguably the most important anti-pattern in this whole checklist — is sourced to a third-party blog (Jobloo), not to Greenhouse's own documentation. It should be treated as a plausible, citable claim worth designing against, not as a confirmed fact about Greenhouse's current system.
5. **Compliance claims are thin outside one vendor.** Only RChilli's certification stack appears in the research at all (Section 7); this says nothing about whether Textkernel, Daxtra, or HireAbility meet or fail to meet the same bar — it may just mean they don't market it the same way.
6. **Adversarial/edge-case behavior is entirely undocumented.** No vendor publishes how their parser handles intentionally malformed or adversarially-crafted resumes (`engines-textkernel-daxtra.md`, Cross-Company Gaps).

**Practical next step implied by these gaps**: build the synthetic/ground-truth resume test set called for in MISSION.md (PDF + DOCX, varied layouts including multi-column, varied "text-layer quality" including at least one deliberately scanned/flattened sample) before treating any number in Section 3 of this checklist as a target to beat. Until then, this checklist tells you *what to measure and what questions to ask a vendor*, not *what score counts as passing*.
