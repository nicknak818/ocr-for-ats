The OCR compute ladder

Open-source ways to read a resume, cheapest rung first. Facts checked 12 Sep 2026. Scores and speeds are the sources' claims until our own lab reproduces them.

Climb only as high as the document forces you. Most resumes arrive as born-digital PDF or DOCX files that already contain text, so the first move is to read that text layer, not to run OCR. What breaks ATS parsing is usually reading order (two columns, sidebars, header contact blocks), not reading characters. Alibaba's deployed resume parser reads the PDF text layer and runs OCR only on image regions.

What leaderboards won't tell an ATS builder

  1. Different documents. OmniDocBench rewards formulas, tables and dense pages. Resumes are sparse, and they fail on column order, sidebars, icons and header contact blocks.
  2. Different metric. Leaderboards score whole-page text similarity. An ATS needs field-level accuracy (name, email, dates, employer), measured by aligning entities and computing field F1, as the Alibaba study does.
  3. Licence can veto the winner. AGPL (PyMuPDF), OpenRAIL-M commercial limits (Chandra) and custom terms (MinerU) all apply. Check before you benchmark.
  4. Bigger isn't better. Specialists at 0.8 to 1.2B lead OmniDocBench v1.6. In the Alibaba study a fine-tuned 0.6B model beat a frontier LLM on resume F1 (0.964 vs 0.919) and was about 15× faster (1.54 s vs 22.71 s).

The two benchmarks named here

OmniDocBench (v1.7 since Apr 2026) scores whole-page parsing: text edit distance, table TEDS, formula CDM, reading-order edit distance. olmOCR-Bench runs 8,413 pass/fail unit tests over 1,403 PDF pages, including multi-column reading order, headers and footers, and old scans.
